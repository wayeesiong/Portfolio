01 · Risk Management Question

How much downside risk does the portfolio carry, and how should it be hedged?

The project constructs a hypothetical $1 billion portfolio allocated equally across five Hong Kong and China-related equity indices. Each index represents 20% of the initial portfolio.

The objective was not only to quantify market risk, but also to evaluate how alternative derivative strategies change the portfolio's exposure to adverse market movements.

How do Historical Simulation, Delta-Normal and Monte Carlo approaches differ in their estimates of portfolio tail risk, and what are the trade-offs between remaining unhedged, using index futures and purchasing index put options?
02 · Portfolio Construction

A diversified Hong Kong / China equity-index portfolio

The initial $1 billion portfolio was divided equally across five equity indices. Historical index values were converted into U.S. dollar terms using the USD/HKD exchange rate before portfolio returns and risk measures were calculated.

03 · Methodology

From market exposure to tail-risk measurement and hedging

01

Construct Portfolio

Allocate $200 million to each of the five indices and convert index values into U.S. dollar terms.

02

Estimate Market Beta

Regress portfolio daily returns against S&P 500 returns to estimate systematic market sensitivity.

03

Historical Simulation

Estimate 99% VaR and Expected Shortfall directly from the historical portfolio loss distribution.

04

Delta-Normal VaR

Apply a parametric approach based on portfolio mean, volatility and a normal-distribution assumption.

05

Monte Carlo Simulation

Generate 1,000 simulated portfolio-loss outcomes and estimate VaR and Expected Shortfall from the simulated distribution.

06

Evaluate Hedging Strategies

Compare an unhedged portfolio with E-mini S&P 500 futures and put-option hedging under multiple market scenarios.

04 · Market Exposure

Low S&P 500 beta, but substantial risk remains

Regression analysis produced a portfolio beta of approximately 0.2554 against the S&P 500. This indicates relatively low sensitivity to movements in the U.S. equity benchmark.

However, the regression R² was only approximately 1.31%. Therefore, S&P 500 movements explain only a small proportion of the portfolio's daily return variation. The portfolio may appear defensive relative to the S&P 500 while still being exposed to significant regional, sector and cross-market risk.

05 · VaR & Expected Shortfall

VaR estimates converge near $40 million — tail-loss estimates do not

The three methodologies produced similar one-day 99% VaR estimates, ranging from approximately $39.88 million to $41.37 million.

Expected Shortfall tells a different story. Historical Simulation produced a substantially larger ES because severe losses observed in the historical sample remain directly represented in the tail of the empirical distribution.

06 · Distribution Risk

Negative skewness and fat tails challenge normal assumptions

Across 316 observed portfolio values, the distribution displayed negative skewness of approximately -1.30 and kurtosis of approximately 10.88.

These characteristics indicate an asymmetric and leptokurtic distribution in which extreme downside outcomes occur more frequently than would be suggested by a normal distribution. This is particularly important when interpreting parametric risk measures.

07 · Hedging Strategy

Futures neutralise market exposure; put options preserve upside

Three portfolio strategies were evaluated: remaining unhedged, shorting E-mini S&P 500 futures, and purchasing E-mini S&P 500 put options.

Strategy Protection Upside Trade-off
Unhedged Low Full Retains full market exposure
Futures Hedge High Very limited Market exposure largely neutralised
Put Option Hedge High Retained Requires an upfront option premium

Under the futures strategy, gains and losses on the hedge approximately offset changes in the underlying portfolio, keeping its value close to $1 billion across the scenario range.

The option strategy instead provides asymmetric protection. A slightly out-of-the-money E-mini S&P 500 put with a 5,550 strike was evaluated, with a total premium of approximately $1.87 million. This creates downside protection while retaining participation in favourable market movements.

08 · Limitations

Model output is only one part of the risk-management decision

The analysis illustrates several practical limitations that should be considered when interpreting the results.

Basis risk The portfolio contains Hong Kong / China equity exposure, while the hedge uses S&P 500 derivatives. The low regression R² suggests that the hedge may not capture all major sources of portfolio risk.
Historical dependence Historical Simulation depends on the selected observation window and may not capture future market regimes.
Distribution assumption Delta-Normal VaR assumes normally distributed losses despite evidence of negative skewness and high kurtosis.
Simulation risk Monte Carlo results depend on modelling assumptions and the number of simulated scenarios.
Implementation Transaction costs, liquidity, margin requirements, collateral and regulatory constraints affect real-world hedge performance.
