01 · Risk question

How much market risk does the portfolio carry, and how should it be hedged?

This project evaluates the market-risk exposure of a $1 billion Hong Kong and China equity-index portfolio. The analysis compares several approaches to Value at Risk and Expected Shortfall before assessing how futures and put options can be used to control downside risk.

Core objective: quantify potential portfolio losses under different risk models and determine how alternative derivative hedges change the portfolio's downside protection, cost and upside participation.
02 · Methodology

From portfolio exposure to downside-risk management

The analysis combines market-beta estimation, multiple VaR approaches and scenario-based derivative hedging.

STEP 01

Construct portfolio

Build a $1 billion portfolio using five Hong Kong and China equity indices with equal 20% allocations.

STEP 02

Estimate market exposure

Regress portfolio returns against the S&P 500 to estimate systematic market sensitivity through beta.

STEP 03

Measure tail risk

Estimate 99% one-day VaR and Expected Shortfall using historical simulation, delta-normal and Monte Carlo approaches.

STEP 04

Compare hedges

Evaluate an unhedged portfolio against E-mini S&P 500 futures and put-option hedges under different market scenarios.

03 · Portfolio & data

A diversified regional equity-index portfolio

  • Portfolio value: $1 billion.
  • Portfolio composition: five Hong Kong and China equity indices, each allocated 20%.
  • Observation period: 316 market days used in the risk analysis.
  • Market benchmark: S&P 500 for portfolio-beta estimation and hedging analysis.
  • Risk measures: 99% Value at Risk and Expected Shortfall.
  • Hedging instruments: E-mini S&P 500 futures and put options.
04 · Key findings

Different risk models produced similar VaR estimates but very different tail-loss estimates.

$39.88m Historical VaR

Historical simulation produced the lowest 99% one-day VaR, equivalent to approximately 3.99% of the $1 billion portfolio.

$41.37m Delta-Normal VaR

The delta-normal method produced the highest VaR estimate, reflecting its assumption of a normally distributed loss process.

$39.93m Monte Carlo VaR

Monte Carlo simulation generated a VaR estimate close to the historical result while allowing simulated loss outcomes to be examined.

β = 0.255

The portfolio exhibited relatively low sensitivity to S&P 500 movements, although its return distribution still displayed substantial downside-tail risk.

05 · Tail-risk interpretation

Low market beta does not mean low extreme-loss risk.

The portfolio beta of approximately 0.255 suggests relatively low sensitivity to movements in the S&P 500. However, the portfolio-value distribution was negatively skewed and highly leptokurtic, indicating heavier downside tails than a normal distribution would imply.

This distinction matters because beta measures systematic market sensitivity, while VaR and Expected Shortfall focus on the size of potential portfolio losses. A portfolio can therefore have relatively low market beta while still experiencing meaningful tail-risk exposure.

06 · Hedging comparison

Futures maximise protection; options preserve upside.

Unhedged portfolio

Retains full participation in market gains but remains fully exposed to losses when equity markets decline.

Futures hedge

Provides the strongest and lower-cost protection by offsetting portfolio movements, but sacrifices the opportunity to benefit from favourable market gains.

Put-option hedge

Places a floor on downside exposure while preserving upside participation, but requires payment of an option premium.

Strategy depends on outlook

Futures are more appropriate when downside protection is the dominant objective, while options provide greater flexibility when large movements in either direction remain possible.

07 · Limitations

Risk estimates depend on model assumptions.

  • Historical VaR reflects observed market outcomes and may fail to capture extreme events that did not occur within the historical sample.
  • Delta-normal VaR assumes a normal loss distribution even though the observed portfolio distribution was negatively skewed and fat-tailed.
  • Monte Carlo results depend on the assumptions used to generate simulated return scenarios.
  • Hedging effectiveness is sensitive to portfolio beta, contract pricing, transaction costs, liquidity and changes in market conditions.
08 · Recruiter takeaway

What this project demonstrates

This project demonstrates the ability to quantify market risk using multiple VaR frameworks, distinguish systematic exposure from tail risk, compare derivative hedging alternatives and translate quantitative risk estimates into practical risk-management decisions.
