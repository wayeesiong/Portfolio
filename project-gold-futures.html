Macroeconomic Determinants of U.S. Gold Futures Prices

A ten-year econometric study examining how U.S. macroeconomic conditions relate to gold futures prices, using OLS, diagnostic testing, HAC/Newey-West estimation and an AR(1) specification.

In brief

What the analysis found

01

Monetary conditions were important drivers of gold futures. Money supply and interest rates were positively associated with gold futures prices, while industrial production showed a negative relationship.

02

Past gold prices carried substantial explanatory power. The previous month's gold futures price remained highly significant, indicating strong persistence in monthly price dynamics.

03

The robust specification materially improved the model. Adding the AR(1) term and HAC/Newey-West estimation resolved the autocorrelation issue and increased model explanatory power to R² = 0.967.

01 · Research question

Which macroeconomic forces matter most for gold futures prices?

Gold is widely used as a store of value, safe-haven asset and portfolio diversifier. This project examines whether U.S. monetary and economic conditions help explain movements in gold futures prices over a ten-year period.

Core objective: identify which macroeconomic variables are statistically associated with gold futures prices and develop a regression specification that remains reliable after accounting for time-series diagnostic issues.
02 · Methodology

From baseline OLS to a robust lagged specification

The analysis begins with a standard multivariate regression before progressively addressing violations of classical OLS assumptions.

STEP 01

Build dataset

Combine monthly gold futures prices with U.S. dollar, inflation, money supply, interest-rate and industrial-production data.

STEP 02

Estimate OLS

Estimate the baseline relationship between gold futures prices and the selected macroeconomic variables.

STEP 03

Run diagnostics

Test for multicollinearity, heteroscedasticity and autocorrelation using correlation, VIF and residual-based diagnostics.

STEP 04

Robustify model

Apply HAC/Newey-West estimation and introduce a first-order lag of gold futures prices to address serial correlation.

03 · Data

Ten years of monthly market and macroeconomic data

  • Gold Futures Price: monthly COMEX gold futures prices.
  • U.S. Dollar Index: measure of dollar strength against major currencies.
  • Consumer Price Index: proxy for U.S. inflation.
  • Money Supply: U.S. M2 monetary aggregate.
  • Interest Rate: Federal Funds Effective Rate.
  • Industrial Production: proxy for real U.S. economic activity.
  • Study horizon: March 2014 to March 2024, with 121 monthly observations.
04 · Key findings

Monetary conditions, economic activity and price persistence matter.

Money supply ↑

M2 was positively associated with gold futures prices and remained statistically significant at the 1% level in the final model.

Interest rates ↑

The Federal Funds Effective Rate showed a positive and statistically significant relationship with gold futures prices.

Industrial production ↓

Higher industrial production was associated with lower gold futures prices, consistent with investors shifting toward productive assets during stronger economic conditions.

Strong price persistence

The previous month's gold futures price was highly significant, with an estimated coefficient of approximately 0.773.

05 · Interpretation

What the results suggest

The positive money-supply relationship is consistent with gold's role as a store of value when monetary expansion raises concerns about currency depreciation. Industrial production shows the opposite pattern: stronger real economic activity can reduce demand for defensive assets as capital shifts toward more productive investments.

The positive interest-rate relationship differs from the conventional opportunity-cost argument for non-yielding gold. One interpretation is that periods of rising rates may coincide with inflationary or economic uncertainty that also increases safe-haven demand. The strong lagged-price coefficient additionally indicates substantial persistence in monthly gold futures dynamics.

06 · Robustness & limitations

Improving the reliability of the specification

  • HAC/Newey-West standard errors were introduced after diagnostic tests identified heteroscedasticity and autocorrelation in the baseline model.
  • Adding the first lag of gold futures prices resolved the remaining autocorrelation, with the Durbin-Watson statistic moving close to 2.
  • The final specification increased R² from approximately 0.925 to 0.967 and reduced the Akaike Information Criterion.
  • The relationships should be interpreted as statistical associations rather than definitive causal effects; geopolitical events, central-bank purchases and other market variables are not fully captured.
07 · Recruiter takeaway

What this project demonstrates

This project demonstrates the ability to build and refine an econometric model, diagnose violations of regression assumptions, apply robust estimation techniques and translate statistical results into market-relevant financial interpretation.
