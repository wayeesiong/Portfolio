Macroeconomic Determinants of U.S. Gold Futures Prices

A ten-year econometric study examining how U.S. macroeconomic conditions relate to gold futures prices, using OLS, diagnostic testing, HAC/Newey-West estimation and an AR(1) specification.

What the analysis found

Monetary conditions were important drivers of gold futures. Money supply and interest rates were positively associated with gold futures prices, while industrial production showed a negative relationship.

01 · Research question

What drives U.S. gold futures prices?

Gold is widely used as a store of value, portfolio diversifier and safe-haven asset during periods of economic uncertainty. This project examines whether major U.S. macroeconomic variables help explain movements in gold futures prices.

The analysis focuses on five potential determinants: the U.S. Dollar Index, Consumer Price Index, M2 money supply, Federal Funds Effective Rate and Industrial Production Index.

Core question: How do major U.S. macroeconomic variables influence U.S. gold futures prices?
02 · Methodology

From initial regression to a more robust specification.

Rather than relying solely on the initial OLS regression, the analysis progressively tested the statistical properties of the model and adjusted the specification where necessary.

01

Estimate initial OLS model

Model gold futures prices as a function of the five selected macroeconomic variables.

02

Diagnose model assumptions

Test for multicollinearity, heteroscedasticity and autocorrelation using VIF, Breusch-Pagan-Godfrey, White, Durbin-Watson and Breusch-Godfrey tests.

03

Apply robust inference

Use HAC / Newey-West standard errors after identifying heteroscedasticity and serial correlation in the initial specification.

04

Refine the dynamic specification

Add the first lag of gold futures prices and compare alternative lag orders using AIC and HQC to identify the preferred final model.

03 · Data

Ten years of monthly market and macroeconomic data.

04 · Key findings

The final model highlights both macroeconomic and price-persistence effects.

+0.0186

Money Supply

M2 money supply is positively associated with gold futures prices and remains statistically significant at the 1% level.

+19.29

Interest Rate

A one-percentage-point increase in the federal funds rate is associated with an approximately USD 19.29 increase in gold futures prices, holding other variables constant.

−9.15

Industrial Production

Higher industrial production is associated with lower gold futures prices in the preferred model.

+0.773

Previous-Month Price

The previous month's gold futures price is strongly significant, indicating substantial persistence in gold futures price movements.

05 · Interpretation

Gold prices reflect both macroeconomic conditions and market persistence.

The final specification suggests that changes in liquidity, monetary conditions and real economic activity are associated with gold futures prices.

Money supply displays a positive relationship with gold futures prices, while industrial production is negatively associated with gold. This is consistent with the idea that stronger productive-sector conditions can reduce the relative appeal of safe-haven assets.

The significant lagged gold futures term also indicates that recent price information contains substantial explanatory power for current prices.

06 · Robustness & limitations

Diagnostics materially changed the interpretation of the model.

The initial OLS specification produced a high R² of approximately 0.925, but diagnostic tests identified both heteroscedasticity and positive autocorrelation.

HAC / Newey-West standard errors were therefore applied to obtain more reliable inference. A lagged dependent variable was subsequently introduced to address the model's dynamic structure.

The preferred GF(-1) specification improved the model R² to approximately 0.967 and produced a Durbin-Watson statistic of approximately 2.019. The Breusch-Godfrey test no longer indicated statistically significant serial correlation.

The analysis remains observational and should therefore be interpreted as evidence of statistical relationships rather than definitive causal effects.
