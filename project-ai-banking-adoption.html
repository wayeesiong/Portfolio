Gen Z Adoption of AI in Banking: Evidence from Malaysia

A quantitative study of 633 Malaysian Gen Z respondents examining how usefulness, ease of use, attitude, perceived risk and trust shape intention to adopt AI-enabled banking services.

In brief

What the analysis found

01

Attitude was the strongest driver of adoption intention. Among the factors analysed, attitude toward AI in banking had the largest overall effect on Gen Z Malaysians' intention to adopt the technology.

02

Usefulness and ease of use were especially important. Perceived usefulness ranked second in overall effect, followed by perceived ease of use, reinforcing the importance of clear benefits and convenient user experiences.

01 · Research question

What drives Malaysian Gen Z to adopt AI-enabled banking?

As banks increasingly integrate artificial intelligence into digital financial services, adoption depends not only on the technology itself but also on how customers perceive its usefulness, convenience, trustworthiness and risk.

Core objective: identify the factors that shape Malaysian Gen Z's intention to adopt AI in banking and determine which factors have the greatest influence on adoption behaviour.
02 · Methodology

An extended Technology Acceptance Model tested with PLS-SEM

The study combines primary survey data with structural equation modelling to test both direct and mediated relationships between technology perceptions and adoption intention.

STEP 01

Design framework

Extend the Technology Acceptance Model by combining perceived usefulness and ease of use with perceived trust and perceived risk.

STEP 02

Collect survey data

Gather primary questionnaire responses from Malaysian Gen Z participants regarding their perceptions of AI-enabled banking.

STEP 03

Validate constructs

Assess reliability, convergent validity, discriminant validity and common-method bias before testing the structural model.

STEP 04

Estimate relationships

Use SmartPLS and PLS-SEM to test path relationships, mediation effects and the relative effect sizes of the adoption factors.

03 · Sample & model

Primary evidence from 633 Malaysian Gen Z respondents

  • Sample size: 633 Malaysian Gen Z respondents.
  • Research approach: quantitative questionnaire-based study.
  • Core framework: extended Technology Acceptance Model.
  • Key constructs: perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, attitude, perceived risk and perceived trust.
  • Analysis: Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling using SmartPLS.
04 · Key findings

Attitude emerged as the strongest driver of adoption intention.

#1 Attitude

Attitude toward AI in banking had the largest overall effect on adoption intention, making it the most important factor in the model.

#2 Perceived usefulness

Perceived usefulness had both direct and indirect positive effects on adoption intention and was the strongest underlying influence on attitude.

#3 Ease of use

Perceived ease of use supported adoption indirectly by improving both perceived usefulness and attitudes toward banking AI.

Trust ↑ · Risk ↓

Greater trust improved attitudes toward banking AI, while higher perceived risk weakened them. Risk nevertheless had the smallest overall effect among the factors examined.

05 · Interpretation

Perceived benefits matter more than perceived risks.

The results suggest that Gen Z Malaysians are more likely to adopt banking AI when they believe the technology is useful, convenient and easy to integrate into their financial activities. These perceptions shape a more favourable overall attitude, which ultimately drives adoption intention.

Trust remains important because users must believe that AI-enabled financial services are reliable, secure and capable of protecting their information. At the same time, perceived risk had a comparatively smaller total effect, suggesting that attractive benefits can partially outweigh concerns about uncertainty and security.

06 · Practical implications

How banks can encourage greater AI adoption

Make the benefit obvious

Banking AI should demonstrate practical value through personalised financial support, faster service and relevant customer benefits.

Reduce friction

Simple interfaces, intuitive workflows and clear onboarding can strengthen perceived ease of use and improve customer attitudes.

Build visible trust

Banks should communicate clearly how AI systems protect customer information, manage privacy and maintain service reliability.

Manage risk transparently

Security safeguards and governance should be communicated openly so customers understand how financial, privacy and cybersecurity risks are controlled.

07 · Limitations

Interpreting the results within the study's scope

  • The research focuses specifically on Malaysian Gen Z consumers, so the findings may not generalise to other countries or age groups.
  • Convenience sampling introduces potential sampling bias and may limit the representativeness of the respondent pool.
  • The study examines adoption intention rather than observed long-term usage behaviour.
  • Perceptions of banking AI may change as the technology becomes more mature and widely adopted.
08 · Recruiter takeaway

What this project demonstrates

This thesis demonstrates the ability to independently design a quantitative research project, collect and analyse primary data, apply PLS-SEM, validate a structural model and translate statistical findings into practical recommendations for banks and FinTech stakeholders.
