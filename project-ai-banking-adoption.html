Gen Z Adoption of AI in Banking: Evidence from Malaysia

Analysed primary survey data from 633 Malaysian Gen Z respondents using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling (PLS-SEM) to examine how usefulness, ease of use, attitude, perceived risk and trust influence the intention to adopt AI-enabled banking services.

01

Research Question

As AI becomes increasingly embedded in financial services, understanding what drives customers to adopt AI-enabled banking becomes important for banks undergoing digital transformation.

What factors influence Malaysian Gen Z's intention to adopt AI in banking?

The research extends the Technology Acceptance Model by examining usefulness and ease of use alongside attitude, perceived risk and perceived trust.

02

Research Framework

The study combines the Technology Acceptance Model with additional risk- and trust-related variables to analyse technology-adoption behaviour in a financial-services context.

TAM

Technology Acceptance

Usefulness and ease of use form the foundation of the technology-adoption framework.

Risk

Prospect Theory

Perceived risk captures the potential downside consumers associate with adopting AI-enabled financial services.

Trust

Financial Technology

Trust reflects confidence in the reliability, safety and privacy of banking AI.

03

Data Collection

Primary cross-sectional data were collected using a structured questionnaire targeted at Malaysian Gen Z respondents.

Component Details
Main Sample 633 Malaysian Gen Z respondents
Pilot Study 34 respondents
Measurement 5-point Likert scale
Design Quantitative, cross-sectional survey
Sampling Convenience sampling
04

Methodology

1

Questionnaire Design

Designed constructs measuring usefulness, ease of use, attitude, risk, trust and adoption intention.

2

Pilot Reliability Test

Conducted a pilot study and assessed internal consistency using Cronbach's alpha before full-scale data collection.

3

Measurement Model

Evaluated indicator reliability, internal consistency, convergent validity and discriminant validity.

4

Multicollinearity

Assessed inner-model Variance Inflation Factors before interpreting the structural relationships.

5

PLS-SEM

Estimated structural relationships among the latent constructs using SmartPLS 4.

6

Bootstrapping

Used 5,000 bootstrap subsamples to assess path significance, mediation relationships and hypotheses.

08

Implications for Banks

Demonstrate value Make the practical benefits of AI-enabled banking visible and relevant to younger customers.
Simplify adoption Reduce friction through intuitive interfaces and clear user guidance.
Build trust Communicate reliability, privacy safeguards and security controls clearly.
Shape attitudes Adoption strategies should improve the overall customer perception of AI banking rather than focusing only on risk.
09

Limitations

Sampling Convenience sampling introduces potential selection bias.
Representativeness The respondent pool is heavily concentrated among tertiary students.
Model specification Other behavioural variables may influence adoption but are not included in the extended TAM.
Cross-sectional design The study captures perceptions at one point in time and should not be interpreted as establishing strong causality.
Generalisability Findings should not automatically be generalised beyond Malaysian Gen Z respondents.
