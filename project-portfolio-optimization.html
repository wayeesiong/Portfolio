01 · Research question

How should investors balance portfolio risk, return and market exposure?

This project applies portfolio theory to examine how different allocations between Berkshire Hathaway and AT&T affect portfolio risk and return, before extending the analysis to systematic market risk across a broader set of U.S. stocks.

Core objective: identify the minimum-variance and optimal risky portfolios using mean-variance optimisation, then assess market sensitivity through beta estimation.
02 · Methodology

From two-asset optimisation to market-risk estimation

The analysis combines Markowitz portfolio construction with the Single Index Model to evaluate both total portfolio risk and systematic market risk.

STEP 01

Measure risk & return

Calculate average returns, standard deviations, coefficients of variation, covariance and correlation for BRK-B and AT&T.

STEP 02

Build the efficient frontier

Evaluate alternative BRK-B and AT&T portfolio weights to map the trade-off between expected return and portfolio volatility.

STEP 03

Identify optimal portfolios

Determine the minimum-variance allocation and use the Capital Allocation Line to identify the optimal risky portfolio.

STEP 04

Estimate market risk

Regress individual stock returns against S&P 500 returns to estimate beta across 30 stocks using the Single Index Model.

03 · Data

Two-asset optimisation supported by a broader market-risk sample

  • Portfolio assets: Berkshire Hathaway Class B (BRK-B) and AT&T (T).
  • Portfolio analysis: return, volatility, covariance, correlation and efficient-frontier combinations across alternative asset weights.
  • Risk-free rate: 0.2% assumed during the sampling period for Capital Allocation Line analysis.
  • Market-risk analysis: beta estimation across 30 stocks using S&P 500 returns as the market benchmark.
  • Primary tool: Microsoft Excel for portfolio calculations, optimisation and regression analysis.
04 · Key findings

Lower risk favoured BRK-B, while the optimal risky portfolio favoured AT&T.

Minimum variance: 75.92% BRK-B

The lowest-risk portfolio allocated 75.92% to BRK-B and 24.08% to AT&T, producing the minimum portfolio standard deviation.

Optimal risky: 75.52% AT&T

The optimal risky portfolio allocated 24.48% to BRK-B and 75.52% to AT&T, producing the steepest Capital Allocation Line.

Moderate diversification

BRK-B and AT&T had a correlation of approximately 0.461, indicating some diversification benefit but substantial remaining co-movement.

Beta estimates were mostly weak

Across the 30-stock sample, 17 stocks had negative beta and 13 had positive beta, but only three beta estimates were statistically significant at the 95% confidence level.

05 · Interpretation

What the results suggest

BRK-B exhibited lower volatility and a slightly better risk-adjusted profile, making it more attractive when the objective is to minimise portfolio variance. AT&T offered higher return potential but also greater volatility, so the optimal risky portfolio shifted more weight toward AT&T when the objective changed from pure risk minimisation to maximising the risk-return trade-off.

The moderate positive correlation between the two stocks limits diversification efficiency. A portfolio containing lower-correlated or negatively correlated assets could potentially achieve stronger diversification benefits.

06 · Limitations

Interpreting the portfolio and beta estimates cautiously

  • The two-asset portfolio provides a simplified illustration of mean-variance optimisation and does not represent a fully diversified investment universe.
  • Portfolio weights are sensitive to the historical return and volatility estimates used during the sampling period.
  • The assumed 0.2% risk-free rate affects the Capital Allocation Line and therefore the optimal risky portfolio.
  • Most of the 30 estimated beta coefficients were statistically insignificant, limiting confidence in individual market-risk estimates.
07 · Recruiter takeaway

What this project demonstrates

This project demonstrates the ability to translate portfolio theory into practical asset-allocation decisions, construct and interpret efficient frontiers, evaluate risk-return trade-offs and extend portfolio analysis to systematic market-risk estimation using regression techniques.
